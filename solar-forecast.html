How to use this page

This page forecasts how much electricity solar panels in your region will produce over the coming days, so you can run the dishwasher, washing machine or EV charger when the sun is out instead of when it isn’t.

Getting started

  1. Pick your region. East Flanders is selected by default.
  2. Optional: enter the size of your solar installation in kWp (it’s on your inverter or installation papers). Numbers then show in kWh and kW for your own system instead of relative figures.
  3. Read the big time range at the top: that is the best moment of the day to run heavy appliances.
  4. Click another day in the row of day cards to see its forecast. If a better day is coming up, a link under the headline jumps to it.

What you’re looking at

Best window
The longest stretch of the day when expected output is at least 70% of that day’s peak. It is highlighted in green on the chart.
Expected yield
How much energy the whole day should produce. Without a system size it is shown as kWh/kWp, meaning kWh per installed kilowatt of panels. Multiply by your kWp to get your own figure, or enter it above.
Peak
The highest output expected that day, as a share of installed capacity (or in kW once you enter your system size).
Good, Fair, Poor
A quick label that compares the day’s expected yield with a typical clear day for the time of year. Good is a sunny day, Poor is a dark or rainy one. In winter even a Good day produces little in absolute terms.
Day cards
Each card has a small curve of the day’s output, drawn on the same scale for every day, so taller curves mean more sun. An asterisk (*) means the forecast for that day is still incomplete.
Chart
The orange line is the forecast through the day. The light blue band is the likely range: Elia expects the real value to fall inside it about 8 times out of 10. The dashed dark line shows what was actually measured for hours that have passed, and the dashed vertical line marks the current time. Hover or tap the chart to read exact values.

Good to know

The figures are averages for the whole region, based on many installations. Your own panels may produce more or less because of their direction, tilt and shade. Forecasts further ahead are less certain than tomorrow’s, and clouds can shift the best window by an hour or two, so check back on the day itself. The data refreshes automatically every 15 minutes; the Refresh button fetches it right away.

Source: Elia Open Data, PV forecast (ods210), refreshed every quarter-hour. Values are province-wide averages scaled to the capacity Elia monitors; your roof (orientation, tilt, shading) will differ. Day ratings compare the expected yield with a rough clear-day figure for the month.
